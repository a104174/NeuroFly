# V10.8K — Legal ground contact versus flight handoff admissibility

## Conclusion and scope

**No handoff safety defect was found in the two specified cases.** Legal ground contact can place the body outside the narrower destination flight envelope. The sustained predicate explicitly checks destination flight bounds before querying flight clearance or constructing a flight plant. Both contact cases defer takeoff, walk back into admissible geometry, then wait for the additional clearance requirement. Valid handoffs preserve accepted XYZ and heading.

Baseline main/source revision: `130130834d9fe1c67c3b398f094e30f7d887af9c`. Each focused condition was regenerated twice because existing local compact contact records did not retain every subsequent walking interval. Full canonical repeats matched. No controller, engine, evaluator, scientific input, UI or historical artifact was modified.

## Physical envelopes and conventions

| Maximum flight body-anchor Y | not a ground degree of freedom | 0.614 |

`experimentalLocomotion.locomotionBounds()` validates the pinned ground configuration and derives its planar radius from the historical asset bounds about the canonical anchor, scaled into presentation units. Ground limits are `(chamber_dimension - wall)/2 - radius`. Flight `flightBounds()` uses the same chamber with horizontal clearance 0.28 and upper clearance 0.24. The additional horizontal conservatism is approximately 0.0514288894 per side. These are physical integration envelopes, not a new visual bounding box. Standard chamber width/depth/height are 2.4/1.22/0.86; wall thickness is 0.012.

Both models use X/Z planar coordinates, Y up, heading zero along +Z and positive yaw toward +X. The shared grounded value is the body anchor, not a claim that the physical floor surface itself is at Y=0.1103. No coordinate transform or silent Y offset is needed. Heading is retained in unwrapped radians.

`InteractiveNeuralEngine.advance()` clips proposed planar motion to ground limits. `ContinuousNavigationEngine` adds bounded neural and engineered boundary yaw; its velocity/contact checks stop outward motion at an occupied wall. `LiveGroundSession` validates finite accepted state and its actual ground envelope. `FlightEngine` independently validates grounded initialization, then its conservative bounds. Neither envelope was changed.

## Exact predicate and initializer responsibilities

`mixedGroundFlight.sustainedTakeoffEligible()` first requires finite accumulated path ≥0.18, a safe integer count of at least 50 ground intervals, no contact other than FLOOR, and finite XYZ inside `flightBounds()` on all axes. It then calls `walkingTakeoffEligible()` against the destination flight geometry. That shared predicate requires grounded state, zero vertical velocity, exact floor-anchor clearance zero, minimum forward/left/right ray clearance ≥0.06 and ceiling clearance ≥0.36. Its historical 0.05 path requirement is weaker than the sustained caller's 0.18 and therefore does not relax the sustained gate.

There is no separate absolute heading range restriction: heading must be finite, and the geometric rays depend on it. The observer validates finite position/velocity/heading and the accepted-state/time contract. These predicates are not a general validator for arbitrary fabricated motor states: actual ground integration supplies the bounded planar velocity. The final `FlightEngine` initializer additionally enforces finite vector shape, exact floor Y, zero vertical velocity and planar speed ≤0.18 + 1e−12, an empty command schedule and destination bounds. It does not itself require a historical FLOOR contact flag; the coordinator's source/phase, actual ground session and predicate establish the handoff context. The tolerance is the existing speed-validation tolerance, not a new landing-clearance epsilon.

The sustained predicate returns false outside flight bounds **before** calling the flight observer. The coordinator constructs the flight engine only inside the successful ground-predicate branch. Failed initialization would terminate the session; neither regenerated run has a failure. There is no evidence of an invalid coordinator initialization attempt in these cases.

## Why the historical D label is misleading

`mixedFlightHeadingMatrix.summarizeMixedHeadingRun()` adds **any wall contact** to `unsafeCount`, together with rejected commands, non-finite states, bounds violations, invalid handoffs and discontinuities. `classifyHeadingLanding()` maps that combined flag to `D_INVALID_OR_UNSAFE` regardless of successful first landing. It excludes ordinary FLOOR contacts but does not distinguish ground versus flight wall contacts. Thus D is a conservative combined screening category, not evidence that these legal ground contacts violated physical constraints. The historical evaluator and its results remain unchanged.
